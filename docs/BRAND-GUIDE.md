# Loki’s Lab brand guide

## Brand idea

Loki’s Lab helps hands-on builders gain meaning, not AI noise.

It is a testing lab with a newsroom attached: curious enough to try the strange new thing, disciplined enough to publish the setup, failures, and evidence. The publication serves homelab builders, developers, and hardware enthusiasts who want to run useful local models and autonomous agents—not merely follow model announcements.

## Positioning

For people building with local AI, Loki’s Lab is the independent publication that combines current news with reproducible agent benchmarks and practical education. Unlike announcement-driven AI media, it connects claims to hardware, configurations, tasks, costs, and public evidence.

## Promise

We tell readers what happened, what we tested, what worked, what failed, and what it will take to reproduce it.

## Brand pillars

1. **Evidence over theater.** Scores include methodology, coverage, configuration, and inspectable output.
2. **Useful curiosity.** Enthusiasm is welcome; credulity is not.
3. **Local agency.** Help readers own their tools, data, hardware, and operating knowledge.
4. **Open practice.** Publish scripts, schemas, corrections, and privacy-safe evidence whenever possible.
5. **Independent judgment.** Sponsors may enable testing but never purchase results.

## Name and language

- Public name: **Loki’s Lab**
- Domain and handles: **lokislab**
- Use the typographic apostrophe in display copy when practical.
- Do not pluralize “Lab” or use “Loki Labs.”
- Primary descriptor: **Independent local AI fieldwork**
- Primary line: **Gain meaning, not AI noise.**
- Functional descriptor: **Local AI benchmarks, practical guides, and trusted news.**

## Voice

The voice is enthusiastic but journalistic. It sounds like an experienced builder sharing a surprising result at the workbench, then handing the reader the exact test notes.

Write with:

- Clear claims and concrete nouns
- Short, energetic headlines
- Visible uncertainty when evidence is incomplete
- Plain explanations of hardware and software tradeoffs
- Delight in discoveries, including failures
- Dry wit in moderation

Avoid:

- Breathless “everything changes today” language
- Treating vendor claims as findings
- Norse parody in every sentence
- Gatekeeping beginners
- Hiding configuration or coverage behind one score
- Calling an automated aggregation an original report

### Example voice

Weak: “This insane new model absolutely destroys the competition.”

Loki’s Lab: “Gemma led this Mac mini on coverage and score. It was not the fastest model we tested.”

Weak: “The ultimate local AI setup.”

Loki’s Lab: “The best configuration we found under $1,500—for this suite, on this version.”

## Editorial labels

- **Trusted Wire:** external links from an explicit source allowlist
- **Open Net:** broader automated discovery, clearly separated
- **Lab Notes:** original reactions, experiments, and reported pieces
- **Agent Leaderboard:** comparable benchmark results
- **Loki’s Lab 101:** free setup and learning courses
- **Verified:** evidence reviewed or accepted through a trusted tester policy
- **Unverified:** schema-valid public community result not independently confirmed
- **Under Review:** visible result with an active evidence or moderation question

## Visual identity

The design is a modern technology publication with restrained material references to Norse craft: carved geometry, iron, parchment, copper, smoke, and runic rhythm. It should never look like a fantasy-game storefront.

### Logo

The core mark is two capital L forms arranged as a compact symbol. Their geometry may suggest crossed tools, a shield fitting, or paired weapon silhouettes without literally drawing a weapon. The mark must remain readable as two Ls at favicon size.

Rules:

- Keep the double-L mark simple, flat, and one or two colors.
- Prefer a square field for the favicon and social avatar.
- Preserve clear space equal to one stem width on all sides.
- Do not add horns to a helmet; that is pop-Viking shorthand, not the identity.
- Do not use Marvel’s Loki likeness, costume, wordmark, or trade dress in the logo.
- Test the mark at 16px, 32px, and one-color print before approval.

Historical public-domain Loki imagery may appear in feature art when provenance and rights are documented. Modern franchise references belong in occasional editorial copy, not permanent navigation, slogans, merchandise, or core identity. Direct quotations require a case-by-case editorial and rights review.

### Color palette

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| Ink | `#17201F` | Primary text, header bands, authority |
| Parchment | `#ECE5D8` | Primary page ground |
| Paper | `#F7F3EB` | Cards, data surfaces |
| Ember | `#B74627` | Calls to action, score emphasis, active states |
| Copper | `#8E4D31` | Secondary emphasis and section labels |
| Verified | `#2D6953` | Verification state only |
| Iron | `#626A66` | Secondary text |
| Rule | `#AAA194` | Borders and editorial dividers |

Ember should be scarce enough to signal action or a finding. Verification green is semantic and should not become decoration.

### Typography

- Display/editorial: a sturdy high-contrast serif. The launch shell uses Georgia as a system-safe placeholder.
- Interface/body: Geist Sans.
- Data/configuration: Geist Mono.
- Headlines may be large and tightly spaced; body text stays calm and highly readable.
- Use uppercase tracking only for short section labels and status metadata.

### Layout and imagery

- Prefer strong editorial grids, rules, generous margins, and dense but legible data tables.
- Use asymmetry in feature layouts, not in benchmark data alignment.
- Photography should show real machines, workspaces, ports, thermals, terminals, and test artifacts.
- Illustrations may borrow the line weight and interlace logic of historical carving, with modern restraint.
- Charts and scores must prioritize comparison and provenance over decoration.

## Community and trust

Speak to contributors as peers. A status describes the evidence, not the person. “Unverified” never means “untrustworthy,” and “under review” never implies wrongdoing. Corrections are visible, specific, timestamped, and linked to affected results.

## Sponsorship treatment

Use the phrases “Equipment provided by,” “Infrastructure provided by,” or “This segment is sponsored by.” Place the disclosure before or alongside the affected work. Never use “partner score,” allow sponsor logos inside score cells, or imply that providing hardware guarantees coverage.
